Liquid seeds

What if you could spray seeds onto the ground like you’re painting with plants?

What are “liquid seeds”?

Liquid seeds are seeds mixed into a wet, squishy blend that you can spray onto soil. Grown-ups call this hydroseeding. It’s like painting grass onto the ground!

What’s in the spray?

  • Seeds to grow new plants.
  • Mulch: tiny bits of paper or wood that keep the seeds wet.
  • Fertilizer: plant food.
  • Sticky stuff so the seeds do not blow or wash away.
  • Green dye so workers can see where they sprayed.

Where is it used?

Hydroseeding is used on steep hills by roads, at building sites, in places burned by wildfires, and next to rivers.

The good

  • It covers big areas fast.
  • The wet mulch helps seeds sprout.
  • It works on steep hills.

The not so good

  • It costs more than just scattering seeds.
  • It needs special machines or trucks.
  • The soil usually needs to be prepared first.

Is there already a product?

Yes! Stores sell “liquid grass seed” sprays. But the ones we found are made for lawns, not for wild native plants.

Our research questions: Is there a spray made for native seeds? Could families use it safely? We are still finding out. Ask an expert!

Seed rules: 1) Only use seeds of native plants that fit the place. 2) Never plant plant bullies. 3) Do not scatter seeds in parks or wild places without permission. The wrong seeds can cause problems.

Try a mini experiment!

Question: Do seeds sprout better when they are sprayed in a wet mulch mix, or when they are just sprinkled on top?

  1. Get two trays with the same soil. Label them A: sprinkled and B: sprayed mix.
  2. Tray A: sprinkle 20 seeds on top of the soil.
  3. Tray B: mix 20 seeds with shredded paper and water, then spread or spray it on top of the soil.
  4. Make it fair! Use the same amount of sun and water for both trays.
  5. Count the sprouts every 3 days and write them in the table.
  6. Decide: Which tray grew more? What would you change next time?

Tip: Practice first with a fast, easy seed like radish or wheatgrass. When your method works, ask an expert about native seeds.
